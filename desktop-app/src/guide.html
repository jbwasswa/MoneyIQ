Pocket Ledger

User guide · Version 0.6.30 (MVP)

Pocket Ledger helps you see where your money goes and where it is going. You record what you earn and spend, keep track of every account you own (cash, bank, mobile money, loans), set monthly budgets, and follow goals such as an emergency fund or a debt you want to clear. Everything is stored on your own computer.

Why Pocket Ledger existsPocket Ledger is built around financial discipline — because that's what it takes to achieve financial freedom. It helps you know where your money goes, build better spending and saving habits, and work deliberately toward the goals that matter to you, whether that's clearing a debt, building an emergency fund, or saving for something specific. You don't need a goal to benefit from the app; tracking your money well is valuable on its own. But when you do set one, Pocket Ledger keeps it visible and keeps you accountable, because the point isn't just to record your money — it's to help you reach financial freedom.

1. Getting started

Opening the app

Unzip the folder anywhere (for example C:\PocketLedger) and double-click Pocket Ledger.exe. There is nothing to install. Keep the whole folder together. Windows may warn that it protected your PC because the app is not signed yet: choose More info, then Run anyway.

Your data is kept separately from this folder, so you can unzip a newer version over an older one and keep everything.

Sample data

The first time you open Pocket Ledger it shows sample data (marked by a dashed banner) so you can explore. When you are ready to start for real, open Settings → Backup & restore and choose Clear sample data. You can bring the samples back with Load sample data.

Your first five minutes

  1. Choose your base currency. Settings → Currencies. The base currency is the one totals are shown in. Add other currencies and their exchange rates if you use them.
  2. Add your accounts. Accounts → Add account. Give each real wallet, bank account or mobile money account an opening balance.
  3. Look at your categories. Settings → Categories. Rename, add or remove categories and subcategories to match your life.
  4. Record a transaction. Press N.
  5. Set a budget or a goal when you are ready (sections 8 and 9).

2. Finding your way around

The left menu has the main pages: Overview, Transactions, Reports, Budgets, Goals, Accounts and Settings. The green New transaction button is always at the top. The box at the bottom of the menu shows your total across all accounts in the base currency.

Choosing the period

At the top of Overview, Transactions, Reports and Budgets there is a period picker: Day, Week, Month or Year. Next to it are arrows to move to the previous or next period. The label between the arrows names the period you are looking at (for example November 2026). Click the label to jump straight back to the current day, week, month or year.

What you pick decides what the page shows: with Day only that day's transactions, with Year the whole year, and so on.

3. Accounts

An account is any place your money sits: cash in your pocket, a bank account, a mobile money wallet, a savings account, or a loan.

Adding an account

Go to Accounts → Add account and fill in:

  • Name – for example Airtel Money.
  • Type – groups and icons your accounts (Cash, Bank, Savings, Mobile money and so on). You can add your own types in Settings → Account types.
  • Currency – the currency this account holds. It is fixed once the account has transactions.
  • Opening balance – how much was in the account when you started using Pocket Ledger. Use a negative number for money you owe on it.
  • Included in Totals – ticked by default. Untick it to leave this account out of the total tiles and the menu total (useful for, say, money you hold on someone else's behalf).
  • Notes – anything you want to remember.

The totals at the top of Accounts

TileMeaning
Total assetsAll accounts with a positive balance, added together (green).
Total liabilitiesAll accounts with a negative balance, added together (red).
BalanceAssets minus liabilities. Green if positive, red if negative, neutral if zero.

Accounts in other currencies are converted to your base currency at the rates in Settings → Currencies.

Loans

Two account types track loans: one for money other people owe you, one for money you owe. Which types play these roles is set in Settings → Linked categories & types, so you can rename them freely (call them whatever you like – "Money lent", "IOUs", anything) without breaking loan tracking. A loan you owe shows as a negative balance, and it counts as a liability.

Deleting an account

An account can only be deleted when nothing uses it: no transactions, no repeating entries and no goals. The app tells you what is in the way.

4. Recording transactions

Press N (or click New transaction) and choose what kind of entry it is:

  • Expense – money going out.
  • Income – money coming in.
  • Transfer – moving money between two of your own accounts.

Expense and income

Fill in the amount, the account, the category and the date. A subcategory is required when saving, so every entry says clearly what it was for (for example Bills – Internet). You can also add a payee or payer, a project, and a note.

Click the category field and start typing to find a category quickly; hovering over a category previews its subcategories.

A new entry starts with the category, subcategory and account you used last (kept separately for expenses, income and transfers), so repeat entries need fewer clicks. The very first time it starts with the first category in your list. You can change any of them.

Typing big amounts quickly

In the amount box you can type a letter after a number instead of all the zeros:

TypeYou get
K or G (thousand, "grand")2K = 2,000  ·  10K = 10,000
M (million)2M = 2,000,000  ·  1.5M = 1,500,000
B (billion)1.2B = 1,200,000,000
T (trillion)1T = 1,000,000,000,000
H (hundred)1H = 100
HT (hundred thousand)1HT = 100,000

It works with calculations too: 2M+500K gives 2,500,000. Capital or small letters both work. The same shortcuts work in a goal's target amount.

Add Next

If you have several entries to record, use Add Next instead of Save. It saves the entry and opens a fresh one that keeps the same category, so you do not have to pick it again.

Repeating entries

Use Repeat for things that happen regularly, such as rent or a salary. It is a dropdown on every transaction, new or saved, so you can save a transaction as a one-off and make it repeat later: open it, choose how often, and save. The entry itself stays as it is, and the next entry is added on the next due date.

  • Repeat: Does not repeat, Every day, Every week, Every 2 weeks, Every month, Every 3 months, Every year, or Custom (for example every 3 days).
  • On: for weekly entries choose the weekday, every weekday, weekends, or Choose days to tick several. For monthly entries choose the same date each month, the same weekday (for example the 2nd Monday), or the last day of the month.
  • Ends: never, on a date, or after a number of times.

To stop a repeat, open one of its entries and choose Does not repeat. Entries already made are kept. Entries that match a repeating entry show its settings, and changes apply to future entries only.

All your repeating entries are listed under Settings → Recurring transactions. Click a row (or Edit) to change how often, the next date, the end, the amount, category, account or note. Delete asks you to confirm, tells you which entries will no longer be added, and offers Undo. Entries already made stay.

Repeating entries are added only when their date arrives. To see what is coming, open Transactions and use the forward arrow (up to a year ahead). Upcoming entries show greyed with an Upcoming tag. They are a preview: they do not count in totals, balances or reports until the day comes. Click one to change its repeat.

Transfers

Choose the account the money leaves (From) and the account it goes to (To). A Transfer fee can be added if you were charged one.

When the two accounts use different currencies, enter what left one account and what arrived in the other; Pocket Ledger works out the rate. Each account's history stays in its own currency. For example, a USD 100 sale paid into a shilling account appears in the dollar account as USD 100 and in the shilling account as the shilling amount received, with a short note such as From Dollar Account USD 100.00.

TipAmounts in other currencies: when an expense or income is in a different currency from its account, the history line shows Converted from USD 100.00 at 4,049, so you can always see what happened.

Future-dated entries

Entries dated after today are marked Upcoming. In Settings you can choose whether they count in account balances and totals now or only when their date arrives.

5. The Transactions page

This is the full list for the period you have chosen, newest first. Each row shows the date, category, account, and the amount. The right-hand column shows the running balance of the account.

  • Search – press / and type a note, payee or category.
  • Filter – narrow by account, category, project and more. Active filters appear as small chips; use Clear filter to remove them.
  • Open an entry to see it, edit it or delete it.

Clicking an account on the Overview filters Transactions to that account.

6. The Overview

The Overview answers "how am I doing?" for the period you chose. At the top are four tiles: Net (income minus expense), Income, Expense and Savings rate. Each compares with the previous period up to the same point.

Below them are cards:

  • Spending pace – a line chart comparing how much you have spent so far with the previous period at the same point. If your line sits below the dashed one, you are spending less than before.
  • Where it went – your spending split by category.
  • Last six months – income and expense side by side.
  • Net worth trend – your assets, liabilities and balance over six months.
  • Budgets – your four closest budgets.
  • Goals – your next goals by deadline.
  • Recent and Accounts – latest entries and balances.

You can show, hide, reorder and choose the number of columns for these cards in Settings → Overview layout.

7. Reports

Reports lets you analyse your money in more detail. Pick the period, choose Expense, Income or Cash flow, and choose how to group it: by category, subcategory, account, project or payee. You can view it as a pie chart, bars or a trend, and export the result as a CSV file for Excel.

8. Budgets

A budget is a monthly limit for one expense category. Open Budgets, click a category and enter the limit. The limit covers all its subcategories. Leave it empty to remove the budget.

Each bar shows how much of the limit you have used this month. The bar turns amber as you approach the limit and red when you go over. The tiles at the top show your overall spending against all budgets.

Stopping or warning about overspending

In the same dialog, When an expense would go over this limit lets you choose how strict a budget is. Each budget has its own setting:

LevelWhat happens when you save an expense that would go over the limit
Off (the default)Nothing is checked. The bar simply turns red.
Warn me firstA message tells you how far over you would be. Choose Save anyway to go ahead or Cancel to change the entry.
Stop me from savingThe expense is not saved. You can go back and lower the amount, or choose Edit budget to change the limit. There is no override, so if you want to be able to go over, set the budget to Warn instead.

Good to know:

  • The check adds up the whole month of that category, including all its subcategories, for the month the expense is dated in. Expenses in other currencies are converted to your base currency first.
  • It only checks expenses. Income, transfers, repeating entries that are added automatically, and imported or restored data are not checked, so your records always stay complete. They will still show up in red on the Budgets page.
  • Editing an old expense is only checked if the edit makes the overspend bigger.
  • Under Stop, once a category is over its limit, every further expense in it is refused until you raise the budget.
  • A budget card shows Stops overspending or Warns on overspend when one of these is on.

9. Goals

Goals help you work towards something: an emergency fund by a date, a debt cleared by a date, or money saved for something you want to buy. Open Goals in the left menu and click Add goal.

How a goal measures progress

Every goal follows one account. As that account's balance changes, the goal's progress changes by itself. You do not enter progress by hand. To save for a laptop, open a savings account for it and point the goal at that account.

Goal types

A goal has a type. Each type works in one of three ways:

Works likeUse it forProgress
Save up to a targetBuying something, a savings potAccount balance ÷ target
Pay down to zeroClearing a debt or loanHow much of the starting debt has been paid off
Keep topped upAn emergency fundAccount balance ÷ target; the goal stays active

The starting types are Emergency fund, Purchase, Savings and Debt payoff. In Settings → Goal types you can rename them, change their icons, add your own, or delete ones you do not use. A type that goals still use cannot be deleted, and there must always be at least one type of each kind.

Setting up a goal

  • Name and Type.
  • Account – the account the goal follows. A debt goal needs an account with money owed (a negative balance).
  • Target amount – or, for a debt goal, the amount owed at the start. If you leave it empty for a debt, today's amount owed is used.
  • Deadline – optional.
  • Note – optional. A free-text reminder of why the goal matters or anything else about it; it shows on the goal's card once set.

Reading a goal card

The thick bar is your money progress, with the percentage at the right. Under it you see the amounts, a status word, how much time is left and the deadline date.

The small dark tick on the bar marks where you should be by today if you were saving evenly from the day the goal began to its deadline. If the coloured bar is beyond the tick, you are ahead. If it falls short, you are behind.

StatusWhat it means
Ahead / On trackProgress is at or beyond the tick, or within five percentage points of it.
BehindMore than five points short. The card suggests what to put aside, for example Save UGX 922,000 a month to catch up.
OverdueThe deadline has passed and the goal is not reached. Time left shows as Overdue by 3 days.
Reached / ClearedThe target is met or the debt is paid.
Below targetA keep-topped-up goal that reached its target once and then dropped below it. The card says how much to add.

A goal with no deadline shows only the money bar. Time left is shown in days when under 60 days, otherwise in months and days.

When a goal is finished

  • Debt goals complete by themselves when the amount owed reaches zero, and move to Completed.
  • Save goals show a Mark as done button once the target is reached. The app asks rather than closing it, in case you want to keep saving. Marking one done offers an Undo for a few seconds.
  • Keep-topped-up goals stay active so you can see if you slip below target.

Use Show completed under the goals to see finished ones with the date you reached them. You can Reopen or Delete them. An overdue goal is never removed for you: edit it to move the deadline or change the target, or delete it. Deleting a goal always asks you to confirm first, whether from its edit screen or from this list.

The rest of the Settings page holds:

  • Balances – whether future-dated entries count now.

On a short window the items at the bottom drop away so the menu never gets cramped; the Total always stays.

11. Backup, restore, import and export

Automatic backups

Pocket Ledger saves a backup when you close it and keeps the last 10 in Documents\Pocket Ledger Backups. Use File → Back up now at any time, and File → Open backup folder to find them.

Restoring

In Settings → Backup & restore use Restore from text and paste the contents of a backup file. Restoring replaces what is in the app, so make a fresh backup first if unsure.

Importing and exporting

  • Import from CSV – paste text in the layout shown on the page.
  • Export as CSV – save your transactions to open in Excel.

Where your data lives

Your data is a single file on this computer: %APPDATA%\Pocket Ledger\pocket-ledger.db. File → Open data folder takes you there. It is not sent anywhere. If you use more than one computer, move data between them with a backup file.

ImportantBackups are your safety net. Copy a recent backup to a USB drive or cloud folder now and then, so a damaged computer does not take your records with it.

13. Questions and problems

A red message appears at the top of the window

It means the last change could not be saved. Your entry is still on screen but is not stored. Use File → Back up now, then tell the developer exactly what the message says.

My balance looks wrong

Check the account's opening balance first, then look for entries dated in the future (see Settings → Balances). For foreign-currency accounts, check the exchange rates under Currencies.

A total does not include an account

Open the account and check that Included in Totals is ticked.

I cannot delete something

Accounts, categories, subcategories and goal types cannot be deleted while entries or goals still use them. The message says what is in the way. Move or delete those first. A category, subcategory or account type currently picked in Settings → Linked categories & types (as the Transfer fee category, or a loan account type) also cannot be deleted until you choose a different one there.

I made a mistake

Open the entry and edit or delete it. Deleting a goal asks you to confirm, then offers an Undo for a few seconds.

About this version

Version 0.6.30 is the first complete release (MVP) of Pocket Ledger. It covers accounts, transactions, transfers between currencies, budgets, goals, reports, repeating entries and backups. It does not connect to banks or mobile money services, and it does not sync between devices by itself. Please send your suggestions and any problems to the developer.
